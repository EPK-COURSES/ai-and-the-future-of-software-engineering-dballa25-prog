# Personal Reflection

Before researching this topic, I mainly viewed AI coding tools as simple productivity assistants that help programmers write code faster. I already knew tools like ChatGPT and GitHub Copilot were becoming popular, but I did not fully think about how much they are changing the learning process for students, especially first-year Software Engineering students.

One thing that surprised me during this research was how differently students use AI tools. Some students use them responsibly to understand concepts and debug errors, while others rely on them too heavily and skip the actual learning process. This made me realize that AI itself is not necessarily good or bad ,the important factor is how people choose to use it.

I also started thinking more seriously about dependency. It is very easy to become comfortable asking AI systems for answers immediately instead of struggling through problems independently. At first this feels efficient, but over time it may weaken important problem-solving and critical thinking skills that software engineers need.

At the same time, I believe AI tools can genuinely improve learning if they are used correctly. For example, getting explanations in simpler language, reviewing your own code, or understanding why an error happens can help students learn faster and reduce frustration. For beginner programmers especially, this support can make programming feel less overwhelming.

Another thing I found interesting is that AI may actually increase the importance of human skills in software engineering. Even if AI becomes better at generating code, software engineers will still need communication skills, creativity, system design thinking, teamwork, and the ability to evaluate whether solutions are actually correct.

Personally, this research changed my perspective slightly. Before, I mostly saw AI coding tools as shortcuts or productivity tools. Now I see them more as assistants that can either help or harm learning depending on how responsibly they are used.

In the future, I think software engineers will need to learn how to work together with AI tools rather than simply avoid them. Knowing how to ask good questions, evaluate AI-generated answers, and continue thinking independently will probably become important professional skills.

Overall, this research helped me better understand both the opportunities and risks of AI in software engineering education. I think the students who benefit the most from AI will be the ones who continue building strong foundations while using AI as support instead of replacement.
